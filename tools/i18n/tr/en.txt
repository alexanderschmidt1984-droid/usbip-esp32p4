H27|<b>Forgot the password</b>: press <b>RESET 5 times in a row</b> on the board (about every 2&nbsp;s – at least 1&nbsp;s, at most 5&nbsp;s apart) – this resets everything to factory settings. Or erase only the settings over the USB cable, the firmware stays: <code>idf.py -p /dev/ttyACM0 erase-region 0x9000 0x6000</code> (Windows: <code>-p COM3</code> or similar). All settings are lost (including WireGuard and TLS keys).
H28|<b>Open</b>: everyone on the network may use USB/IP and this web UI.
H29|<b>Closed (Allowlist)</b>: only listed participants get access, both to USB/IP <b>and</b> to this web UI. An entry is either an <b>IP</b> or a <b>MAC address</b>, plus a <b>comment</b> so it stays clear whom you gave access.
H30|<b>MAC instead of IP</b>: handy if the PC sometimes gets a different IP via DHCP. Works only for PCs in the <b>same LAN</b> (the board reads the MAC from its ARP table), not across WireGuard or a router. A MAC can be spoofed – it is a convenience, not strong protection. For real protection: password, TLS or WireGuard.

H37|<b>Problem</b>: slow devices (Full/Low Speed) such as a <b>YubiKey</b>, keyboard, mouse or many USB serial adapters do not work behind a USB 2.0 hub. The ESP32-P4's USB library would need a "transaction translator" for that, which it does not have yet. The event log then shows "FS/LS device rejected (no TT support)".
H38|<b>Hub compatibility on</b>: the board's USB port works at Full Speed only (12&nbsp;Mbit/s). Then the hub and all devices behind it run at Full Speed too, and YubiKey &amp; co. work on the hub.
H39|<b>Drawback</b>: USB sticks and card readers then also run at Full Speed only – about <b>1&nbsp;MB/s instead of 5–6&nbsp;MB/s</b>. Without a hub or with only fast devices on the hub: leave it off.

H89|<b>Over the internet</b>: the peer is then a server or router with a public address. Peer endpoint is its domain or IP, e.g. the Fritzbox VPN under Internet → Permit Access → VPN (WireGuard). <b>Peer config snippet</b> shows the matching <code>[Peer]</code> block for the peer. H90|<b>No handshake</b> ("connecting · no handshake"): usually the PC's firewall blocks UDP 51820 or a key is mixed up. H91|<b>Power save</b> (on by default): without an attached device the CPU runs at 100&nbsp;MHz instead of 400&nbsp;MHz. As soon as a device is attached it goes to full clock, so transfers do not get slower. Unticked, the board runs at 400&nbsp;MHz all the time. Takes effect immediately, without a restart. When it is on, the green leaf is shown at the bottom left.
H92|<b>Restart</b>: restarts the board. Attached USB devices are disconnected on all PCs and must be attached again with <code>attach</code> afterwards.
H93|<b>Factory Reset</b>: erases <b>all</b> settings (network, password, ACL, device filter, WireGuard and TLS keys) and restarts. Afterwards DHCP without a password applies.
H94|<b>Reset without the web UI</b>: press the <b>RESET</b> button on the board <b>5 times in a row</b>, about every 2&nbsp;s (at least 1&nbsp;s, at most 5&nbsp;s apart; faster presses interrupt the start before it is counted). On the 5th start the board erases all settings like Factory Reset. Power off/on counts the same. A single reset or power failure does nothing; crashes are not counted. The BOOT button cannot be used for this (pressed at power-on = flash mode, in operation Ethernet uses the pin).
H95|<b>Firmware update</b>: over the USB cable with <code>idf.py -p /dev/ttyACM0 flash</code> (Windows: <code>-p COM3</code> or similar). The settings are kept.
